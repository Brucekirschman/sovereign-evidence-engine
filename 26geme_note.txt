 If 26geme is as far as this ever goes, and GitHub just stays like this, just one more abandoned project, can that version function? Can it be the artifact?

Yes, it can function—and yes, it can stand on its own as a real, working artifact, even if the repo never gets another commit and development stops right here.
Looking straight at the code in see-0.0.26gemexx.txt (VER = "see-0.0.26geme"), it isn't a dead sketch or a broken toy. It already has the core pieces wired together in a single file:
 * **Notes, files, and minting:** You can type notes, attach files or images, compute SHA-256 hashes (sha256Str / sha256Bytes), build a canonical chain string (buildCanonicalChainStr), generate a timeline HTML doc (timelineDoc), package it with manifest.json, SEIGNIORAGE.txt, and evidence/ entries into a ZIP (makeZip), and trigger a share or download (mint()).
 * **Chain continuity across updates:** see_prev, see_last_hash, see_mint_parent, and see_last_chain stay in localStorage even when see_ver changes.
 * **Manual P2P room plumbing:** It includes STUN servers (ICE_SERVERS), CRLF normalization (cleanSdp), createRoom(), joinRoom(), acceptAnswer(), and sendPeer() over a WebRTC data channel.
### Honest Limits of That Version as a Standalone Artifact
Being real about what it can and cannot do makes the artifact stronger, not weaker:
 * **Manual SDP copy/paste, no QR or auto-signaling:** Getting two units connected requires copying and pasting SDP text between peers (#sdpOut and #sdpIn), and as the built-in note warns, network/NAT/.local address quirks can still block a connection.
 * **Photos aren't restored after reload:** Reloading clears unsaved screen notes/evidence view state—the minted .zip file is the actual saved package.
 * **A couple of rough edges in the script:** There's the ĄCOPIADO! string in the first #copySdp listener before boot() assigns .onclick, and boot() schedules createRoom() after 600 ms.
A project doesn't have to become a sprawling multi-version platform—or stay active on GitHub—to count as an artifact. If 26geme is where this chapter stops, it's a concrete snapshot of what you built on your phone: offline-first, single-file, verifiable via SHA-256 hashes, and made with your boys in mind.
